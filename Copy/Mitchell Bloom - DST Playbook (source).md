## Introduction
When you sell an appreciated asset — real estate, a business, or a concentrated stock position — the IRS is waiting at the finish line. For high-value sales, capital gains tax can take hundreds of thousands, even millions, off the table. Tax law provides options: with proper planning you may be able to defer your capital gains tax liability, preserve liquidity, and earn interest on pre-tax sale proceeds.

## 1. What Is Capital Gains Tax?
- **Short-term gains** (held under one year): taxed at ordinary income rates (up to 37%).
- **Long-term gains** (held over one year): typically 15%–20%, plus a possible 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax.

Selling a property with a $3M gain could trigger $600,000–$800,000+ in taxes. Deferral lets that capital keep working instead of paying the IRS immediately.

## 2. Why Consider Deferral?
Deferring capital gains isn't about avoiding taxes — it's about timing and control.
- **Preserve Liquidity** — keep more money available after a sale to generate income.
- **Compound Growth** — money that would have gone to taxes stays invested longer.
- **Flexibility** — some strategies let you diversify beyond your original asset type.
- **Estate Planning Advantages** — with the right structure, deferred assets may pass more efficiently to heirs.

## 3. Key Deferral Options

### 3A. 1031 Exchange
Sell an investment property and reinvest into another "like-kind" property, deferring the gain.
- **Advantages:** widely recognized/IRS-approved; roll gains forward; keeps equity compounding in real estate.
- **Limitations:** real estate only; strict deadlines (45 days to identify, 180 days to close); must reinvest into real estate, no diversifying into stocks/bonds/other classes.
- **Fits:** real estate investors committed to staying in property long term.

### 3B. Deferred Sales Trust (DST)
Sell a highly appreciated asset, transfer proceeds into a trust managed by a third-party trustee, and receive installment payments over time. You pay capital gains taxes only as you receive payments.
- **Advantages:** broad applicability (real estate, business sales, stocks, more); liquidity while spreading gain recognition; investment flexibility (diversify across asset classes); potential estate planning benefits.
- **Limitations:** more complex legal structure; trustee and setup fees; must be arranged before the sale closes.
- **Fits:** owners of highly appreciated assets seeking liquidity, diversification, or who don't want to stay tied up in real estate.

### 3C. Other Options
- **Installment Sales** — spread gain over years, but carry buyer default risk.
- **Opportunity Zones** — tax incentives for investing in designated communities.
- **Charitable Remainder Trusts (CRTs)** — combine philanthropy with deferral.

## 4. Choosing the Right Strategy
Depends on asset type, goals (liquidity vs diversification vs staying in real estate), timeline, risk tolerance, and estate planning needs.
- Want to stay in real estate → **1031 Exchange**
- Want liquidity and flexibility → **Deferred Sales Trust**
